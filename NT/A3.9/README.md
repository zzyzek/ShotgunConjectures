# Guy A3: the smallest prime with digit sum k does not always end in 99

**Source.** R. K. Guy, *Unsolved Problems in Number Theory*, 3rd ed., Springer 2004, section A3 (p. 16). De Koninck's questions, paraphrased: let p(k) be the
smallest prime whose decimal digits sum to k. Is p(k) ≡ 9 (mod 10) for k > 25, ≡ 99
(mod 100) for k > 38, and ≡ 999 (mod 1000) for k > 59?

**Result.** The second and third parts are false. p(86) = 6999999989, which is ≡ 89
(mod 100) and ≡ 989 (mod 1000). Further failures below k = 180: p(104) = 699999999989 and
p(137) = 3999999999999989. The mod-10 part holds for every 25 < k < 180 (k not a multiple of
3); it is not settled beyond that.

**Argument.** A number with digit sum 86 has at least 10 digits (9·9 = 81 < 86). A 10-digit
number with digit sum 86 is 9999999999 lowered by a total of 4 spread over its digits. In
increasing order these are 5999999999 (all 4 on the leading digit), then the numbers with
leading digit 6 and one further unit taken from one of the nine lower digits: 6899999999,
6989999999, …, 6999999899, 6999999989, 6999999998. The eight numbers before 6999999989 are
composite (factors 7, 6089, 827, 19, 61, 31, 7, 3011) and 6999999989 is prime
(trial division up to its square root, 83666).

**Evidence.**
- Lean (Mathlib): [`A3_9_mathlib.lean`](A3_9_mathlib.lean) proves the whole Result: `A3_9_main`/`A3_9_false` (p(86) = 6999999989, Mathlib `IsLeast`, ≡ 89 mod 100 and 989 mod 1000, so parts 2 and 3 fail), `A3_9_values` (p(104) = 699999999989, p(137) = 3999999999999989, same residues), `A3_9_mod10` (for every 25 < k < 180 with 3 ∤ k, p(k) exists and is ≡ 9 mod 10) and `A3_9_mult3` (no prime has digit sum k for 3 < k, 3 ∣ k). Primality by Pratt certificates; minimality by a kernel enumeration of all smaller numbers with digit sum k (`chkF`, proved sound) with one proper factor each. Generated by `certificate/a39_lean_gen.py` from `certificate/A3_9_mathlib.lean.tmpl` (the table of p(k) and factors is computed there and checked by the kernel). Outside Lean: nothing.
- Lean: [`A3_9.lean`](A3_9.lean) checks the digit sum and residues of 6999999989 and a
  proper factor of each of the eight smaller candidates (core Lean, no axioms).
  Outside Lean: the list of candidates (the counting argument above) and the primality of
  6999999989 (Miller–Rabin with the first 13 prime bases, deterministic below 3.3·10^24;
  kernel trial division to 83666 exceeded the 300 s limit).
- `certificate/a39_check.py` (second reader, own code with `mr.py`): computes p(k) for all
  k < 180 by generating the numbers of each length with digit sum k in increasing order.
  Same three failures as the scan's `quick1.py` (copied).

**Review level.** `lean-proof`: the whole Result (p(86), p(104), p(137) and the mod-10 part for 25 < k < 180) is proved in Lean + Mathlib ([`A3_9_mathlib.lean`](A3_9_mathlib.lean), axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`).
**Prior art.** Known: [T. Müller, Integers 5 (2005) #A10](https://www.emis.de/journals/INTEGERS/papers/f10/f10.pdf) refutes parts 2 and 3 with the same values p(86) = 6999999989, p(104), p(137) = 4·10^15 − 11 (and more); the mod-10 part stays open there, and our check of it for 25 < k < 180 is a finite verification only.
