# Asymptotics from polynomial recurrences

**When it applies.** Limits of ratios, means or probabilities built from sequences that satisfy
polynomial-coefficient recurrences (row sums of hypergeometric triangles, involution numbers, ...),
where singularity analysis or a moment theorem would be heavy in Lean.

**Method.** (1) Get a recurrence from a pointwise identity between summands (write the term as a
polynomial in n over a factorial, e.g. (n+k)…(n−k+1)/(k!(k+1)!), so the identity is `ring` and needs no
creative telescoping); sum it with weights 1, k, ... (2) Look for an exact linear relation between the
quantity of interest and shifts of a simpler sequence (linear algebra over polynomial coefficients on
computed values). (3) Ratio limits: put the ratio x(n) of consecutive terms through the recurrence and
prove an explicit invariant by induction (bounds plus (n+1)·|x(n) − limit| ≤ M, or √n-type bounds for
super-exponential sequences), then squeeze. (4) Alternating inclusion–exclusion sums: bound each term by a
summable sequence and pass to the limit with Tannery's theorem
(`tendsto_tsum_of_dominated_convergence`) instead of the method of moments.

**Certificate.** Mathlib proof; a script checking the recurrences and the invariants on many n.
