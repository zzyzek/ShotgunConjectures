# Cartier-operator automaton for coefficients modulo prime powers

**When it applies.** a(n) = [x^n t^n] F with F = (sum_i N_i e_i) / Q^(p^(a-1)) over ℤ/p^a, where Q, N_i are
polynomials and the e_i are finitely many series with Q^(p^a - p^(a-1)) e_i = sum_j c_ij e_j(x^p, t^p) (e = (1) for a
rational F). Algebraic series reach this form by Lagrange inversion (one-variable substitution x = φ(t)) or by
Furstenberg's diagonal formula; binomial sums sum_k C(αn+βk, ·) directly.

**Method.** The Cartier operator Λ_r (coefficient (i, j) ↦ (pi+r, pj+r)) satisfies a(pn+r) = diag(Λ_r F)(n) and
Λ_r(G · H(x^p, t^p)) = Λ_r(G) · H. Since Q^(p^a) ≡ Q(x^p, t^p)^(p^(a-1)) (mod p^a), the numerators map to
N'_j = Λ_r(sum_i N_i c_ij) of bounded degree: finitely many states. Explore them (script), then check in Lean that the
listed states are closed under the step (`decide` on the list model) and that their values at 0 satisfy the claim.

**Lean.** Library `OpenProblemsLib.AutomaticMod` (guide [lib/docs/automatic-mod.md](../../lib/docs/automatic-mod.md)):
`ry_step`, `diag_cart2`, `automaton_induction`, list model `ps2`, `mul2`, `half2`, `trim2`, `sig2L`.
Worked instance `OpenProblemsLib.HannaMod4` (six states over ℤ/4, basis (1, W) with a lacunary W).

**Pitfalls.**

- Check Q^(p^a) = Q(x^p, t^p)^(p^(a-1)) for the concrete Q by `decide` on lists; it is cheaper than the general
  Frobenius lemma and catches wrong moduli.
- Keep numerators trimmed (`trim2`) so that states compare by list equality.
- The link from the problem's definition to the diagonal is the real work (here Lagrange inversion plus a
  Fibonacci-polynomial identity); the automaton itself is cheap.
