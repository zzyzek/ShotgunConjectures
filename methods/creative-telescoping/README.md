# Creative telescoping certificate

**When it applies.** a(n) = Σ_i w(n, i) with w hypergeometric in (n, i) (ratios w(n+1,i)/w(n,i) and
w(n,i+1)/w(n,i) rational), and a conjectured recurrence Σ_l c_l(n) a(n-l) = 0 with polynomial c_l.

**Method.** Find G(n, i) with Σ_l c_l(n) w(n-l, i) = G(n, i+1) − G(n, i) for every i in the summation
range (Zeilberger's certificate). Sum over i: the left side is the recurrence, the right side telescopes to
G(n, top) − G(n, 0), which is zero at the support boundary. Floors such as ⌊(n+k)/2⌋ go away after
grouping k by parity (A124431: k = n − 2i and k = n + 1 − 2i give one term in i).

**Certificate.** G written as a combination of binomial products with polynomial coefficients (no rational
function), found by linear algebra on the partial sums Σ_{i'<i} F(n, i'); the identity F = ΔG.

**Lean.** Use a binomial on ℤ × ℤ that is 0 off 0 ≤ y ≤ x. The three contiguous relations
C(x−1, y)·x = C(x, y)·(x−y), C(x−1, y−1)·x = C(x, y)·y, C(x, y+1)·(y+1) = C(x, y)·(x−y) hold on all of
ℤ × ℤ. Chain them (`linear_combination (norm := ring_nf)`, which normalises the binomial arguments) to write
every binomial as base × rational function, then one `field_simp; ring` lemma checks the rational identity.
The finitely many (n, i) where a denominator vanishes are either outside the support or small n, checked by
`decide`.

**Pitfalls.**

- Multiplying out by a common denominator inside one `linear_combination` timed out (200000 heartbeats);
  dividing first and proving the rational identity as a separate lemma took about 40 s.
- Natural-number subtraction in binomial arguments breaks the contiguous relations; use ℤ arguments.
